# Board / Leadership AI Briefing Checklist

A five-minute checklist for the executive or compliance lead briefing the board, audit committee, or supervisory committee on the institution's AI posture.

Use it in three passes: **before**, **during**, and **after** the meeting.

---

## Before the meeting

- [ ] **AI Use-Case Inventory is current** — every approved, in-pilot, and prohibited use case has an owner, data class, risk tier, and last-reviewed date.
- [ ] **AI Acceptable Use Standard is on file** — approved by the right committee, with effective date and next-review date.
- [ ] **Three top risks identified** — for each: likelihood, impact, current mitigation, residual risk.
- [ ] **Vendor AI features inventoried** — every core, ancillary, or marketing vendor that has shipped or announced AI features in the last 12 months is listed with a status (allowed / under review / blocked).
- [ ] **Incidents and near-misses logged** — at least one paragraph each, even if zero customer impact.
- [ ] **Regulatory anchors named** — SR 11-7, Interagency TPRM Guidance, ECOA / Reg B (where lending is involved), GLBA, BSA / AML, AIEOG AI Lexicon. Cite the specific ones that touch the use cases on the inventory.

## During the meeting — what to show

1. **One-page snapshot.** Total use cases by tier, total approved tools, total staff trained, last governance review date.
2. **Top three use cases with measurable outcomes.** Volume, cycle time delta, reviewer-edit distance, escalations.
3. **Top three risks and what is being done about each.** No more than three. Boards lose focus past three.
4. **The one decision you need from them today.** Make it explicit. If there is no decision needed, say so.
5. **The one thing you will not do without their approval.** This builds trust faster than anything else.

## During the meeting — what to avoid

- Vendor demos. The board does not need to see a tool.
- AI capability tours. They are not building the system.
- Adjective-heavy language ("powerful", "transformative", "cutting-edge"). Use nouns and numbers.
- Acronyms without expansion on first use.
- Forecasts you cannot defend with a source.

## After the meeting

- [ ] **Minutes capture the decision.** If there was a decision, the minute language matches what was actually approved — not a softer paraphrase.
- [ ] **Action items have owners and dates.** Every one of them. No "team to follow up."
- [ ] **The Use-Case Inventory is updated** with any new approvals, blocks, or risk-tier changes from the meeting.
- [ ] **The Acceptable Use Standard is updated** if policy moved.
- [ ] **The next briefing date is on the calendar** — at minimum quarterly until governance is mature, then semi-annual.
- [ ] **One paragraph circulated to the executive team** summarizing what changed.

```
AI Posture — as of [date]
─────────────────────────────────────────
Approved use cases:           [n]
In pilot:                     [n]
Blocked:                      [n]
Approved tools:               [n]
Vendors with AI features:     [n]
Staff trained (current cert): [n] of [total]
Last policy review:           [date]
Next governance review:       [date]

Top three risks
  1. [risk]  —  [mitigation]  —  [residual: low/med/high]
  2. [risk]  —  [mitigation]  —  [residual: low/med/high]
  3. [risk]  —  [mitigation]  —  [residual: low/med/high]

Decision requested today
  [one sentence]

Won't do without you
  [one sentence]
```
