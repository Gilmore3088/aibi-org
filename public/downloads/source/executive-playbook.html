Section 1 · Executive snapshot

The operating principle

Leadership does not need to pick every tool. Leadership must name the outcomes, approve the guardrails, and require evidence before scale.

AI adoption inside a financial institution becomes risky when the strategy is implicit. Teams experiment, vendors pitch, staff improvise, and the board receives anecdotes instead of a controlled view of progress. The executive role is to turn AI from a collection of experiments into an operating program with ownership, boundaries, metrics, and review cadence.

The risk line

Executive AI work is usually low-risk when it frames direction, compares options, or prepares internal discussion. It becomes higher risk when it approves a vendor, sets customer-facing policy, affects staffing, or reports operational return without evidence. Treat those outputs as board-ready only after documented human review.

Section 3 · Governed adoption model

From scattered pilots to a governed program

  1. Frame. Name the one or two outcomes AI should move this year and the single accountable owner. Artifact: Adoption thesis.
  2. Guardrail. Approve the data-safety tiers, the human-review rule, and the approved-tool list as policy. Artifact: Signed guardrails.
  3. Measure. Pick two metrics - time saved and correction rate - and require them on every pilot. Artifact: Pilot scorecard.
  4. Review. Run a quarterly review: what scaled, what stopped, where the risk now sits. Artifact: Quarterly AI review.

Executive checklist

  • One accountable owner named for AI adoption.
  • Data-safety and human-review rules approved as policy.
  • Approved-tool list published to all staff.
  • Every pilot reports time saved and correction rate.
  • Board has seen the current risk posture.
  • A decision to scale or stop is made on evidence, not vibes.

30-day leadership path

  1. Week 1: Pick the accountable AI owner and one business outcome.
  2. Week 2: Approve the safe-use guardrails and publish the approved-tool path.
  3. Week 3: Require every active pilot to submit the same two metrics.
  4. Week 4: Run a leadership review and choose which pilot scales, pauses, or stops.
