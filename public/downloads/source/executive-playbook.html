Board packet · decisions to minute

Board-ready starter language

Motion 1 · AI scope

Board-ready starter language

"Management is authorized to pursue AI use cases that improve internal efficiency, documentation, training, customer-service preparation, and workflow support, subject to the institution's approved data line, human-review requirements, vendor oversight, and evidence-retention standards. AI use in or near credit, fraud, AML, account closure, pricing, eligibility, or adverse action requires separate review before launch."

Motion 2 · Data line

Board-ready starter language

"Customer non-public information, transaction-level records, authentication data, SAR-related information, examiner correspondence, employee personnel records, and confidential legal or compliance materials may not be entered into any public or personal AI tool. Sensitive data may be used only in an approved environment with documented contractual, security, logging, and retention controls."

Motion 3 · Owner and cadence

Board-ready starter language

"The [COO / CRO / designated executive] is accountable for AI governance. The AI Governance Committee will maintain the inventory, approve use cases, oversee vendors and incidents, and report to the Board or Risk Committee quarterly."

Regulatory posture

Use existing obligations without over-claiming

Current model-risk guidance, including SR 26-2 where applicable, should be considered when AI supports quantitative, customer-impacting, regulated, or model-like decision processes. Generative AI used for drafting, summarization, research, or workflow support should still be governed through data boundaries, approved tools, human review, vendor oversight, evidence retention, and incident response.

AI used in or near credit must support specific, accurate adverse-action reasons and be monitored for protected-basis outcome gaps, proxy-variable risk, explainability, and governance concerns. Do not rely on opaque AI output where the institution cannot independently explain the credit result.

AreaExecutive control
Credit / ECOA / Reg BSpecific adverse-action reasons, file traceability, explainability, and monitored outcome gaps.
Model-like processesSR 26-2 where applicable; validation and monitoring scaled to risk, size, complexity, and use.
Vendor AIInteragency TPRM: due diligence, contract terms, data use, monitoring, exit, and incident notice.
Data protectionGLBA / safeguards expectations, data classification, approved environments, logging, and retention.
Board oversightInventory, owner, risk tier, incidents, pilot metrics, and decisions recorded in minutes.

Copy the Lakeshore Pattern

One example your executive team can imitate

Lakeshore Community Bank is a $900M-asset community bank with 140 employees. It chooses one internal lending-operations pilot and deliberately keeps AI away from the credit decision.

ElementLakeshore decision
OutcomeReduce loan-document review time without affecting eligibility, pricing, approval, or adverse action.
Data lineCustomer NPI only in the contracted enterprise AI environment; no public or personal AI tools.
OwnerCOO owns the pilot; Compliance and Lending Ops review outputs.
Pilot90-day document-summary pilot, fixed budget, time-saved and correction-rate thresholds.
EvidenceInventory row, pilot memo, reviewer log, correction-rate report, incident log, board dashboard.
Governance and defensibility are the same work done once: scope, data line, owner, pilot, evidence.
Pilot memo

Authorize one pilot with a kill switch

A pilot does not need to be profitable in 90 days. It needs to prove whether the use case is worth scaling.

ROI scorecard

Prove value without overstating it

The CFO test is simple: did the pilot reduce measurable work, preserve quality, and avoid new risk? Count license, integration, oversight, and human-review time. Do not count "AI enthusiasm" as ROI.

Executive rule

A good pilot may be a "not yet." The value is knowing with evidence, not proving the purchase was right.

90-minute sprint

Next step: Run the 90-Minute Executive AI Governance Sprint

In one session, define your AI scope, data line, accountable owner, first pilot, kill switch, and first board dashboard.

  1. First 15 minutes: name the business outcome and what is out of scope.
  2. Next 15 minutes: write the data line and approved-tool boundary.
  3. Next 15 minutes: name the accountable owner and committee cadence.
  4. Next 20 minutes: draft the first pilot memo, success metric, and stop triggers.
  5. Next 15 minutes: fill the first dashboard lines and evidence index.
  6. Final 10 minutes: decide what the board must approve, note, or direct.
By the end, leadership should have a board-ready decision set, not a list of AI ideas.
Sources and adaptation

Govern the program, not the prompt

The executive's job is not to master the technology. It is to make sure the institution uses AI on purpose: with a stated scope, a data line, a named owner, funded and bounded experiments, honest metrics, and a board that can see all of it.
