Section 1 · Executive snapshot

The operating principle

AI training is not complete when staff hear the rules. It is complete when each role can practice safely and readiness is visible.

AI adoption fails when training stays generic. Staff need to know which AI tasks are safe for their role, which data boundaries apply, which approved tools to use, where to escalate, and how to practice without real customer data. Training and HR make readiness observable instead of assumed.

Section 2 · Immediate training use cases

Where training and HR can use AiBI immediately

Build a role-specific training path

Turn generic AI awareness into the two or three things a given role needs to do safely.

Artifact: Role training path.

Write a safe-use one-pager

Give every new hire the green/yellow/red data rules and the approved-tool list on one sheet.

Artifact: Safe-use reference.

Create practice scenarios

Draft realistic, fictional scenarios staff can rehearse in the sandbox - never real customer stories.

Artifact: Scenario pack.

Track who is ready

Define what "trained" means per role and record who has cleared the bar.

Artifact: Capability tracker.

The risk line

Training examples must use fictional or sanitized scenarios. Do not use real customer stories, complaint records, employee performance details, medical information, or live personnel records in AI practice prompts.

Section 3 · Capability operating model

From one-time training to sustained capability

  1. Scope. For one role, name the two or three AI tasks they should be able to do safely. Artifact: Role capability spec.
  2. Build. Assemble the path: the rules, the examples, and a sandbox exercise tied to real role work. Artifact: Role training path.
  3. Run. Deliver it to one cohort. Capture where people get stuck and what questions recur. Artifact: Cohort feedback log.
  4. Track. Record who has cleared the bar, refresh on a cadence, and feed gaps back into the path. Artifact: Capability tracker.

Readiness checklist

  • Training is tied to specific role tasks, not general awareness.
  • Green/yellow/red data rules are covered explicitly.
  • Approved-tool list is part of every path.
  • Practice uses fictional scenarios, never real customer data.
  • "Trained" is defined and measurable per role.
  • There is a named person to ask when staff get stuck.

30-day enablement path

  1. Week 1: Pick one role and define the two AI tasks they should safely perform.
  2. Week 2: Build the role path with data rules, approved tools, and one sandbox exercise.
  3. Week 3: Run a small cohort and record where the path breaks.
  4. Week 4: Publish the path and start tracking capability by role.
