Section 1 · Executive snapshot

Role readiness, not generic awareness

AI training is complete when each role can practice safely, show the skill, and know when to stop.

AI adoption fails when training stays generic. Staff need to know which AI tasks are safe for their role, which data boundaries apply, which approved tools to use, where to escalate, and how to practice without real customer data. Training and HR make readiness observable instead of assumed.

Training Data Boundary Card

Do not use real customer stories, complaint records, employee performance details, medical information, or live personnel records in AI practice prompts.

AI may support training-path design, fictional scenarios, role practice, and readiness tracking. AI may not rank employees, evaluate performance, recommend discipline, screen applicants, determine accommodations, infer protected characteristics, or make employment-related decisions unless the use case has been separately reviewed and approved by HR, Legal, Compliance, and Information Security.

Section 2 · Readiness definition

What AI-ready means

  • Can classify a prompt by data risk.
  • Can choose the approved tool.
  • Can use a role prompt safely.
  • Can remove prohibited data.
  • Can mark uncertain output [VERIFY].
  • Can identify the human review point.
  • Can escalate a red use case.
  • Can complete a fictional scenario.
  • Can pass manager review.

Section 4 · Practice and tracking

Safe-Use One-Pager

  • Use only approved tools.
  • Remove prohibited data before prompting.
  • Use fictional scenarios for practice.
  • Mark uncertain output [VERIFY].
  • Route customer-facing, credit-impacting, employment-related, or regulated output to human review.
  • Escalate red use cases before trying them.

Section 6 · 30-day enablement runbook

Build one role path and prove it works

By day 30, publish one role capability spec, one safe-use one-pager, three fictional scenarios, one sandbox exercise, a manager sign-off card, a capability tracker, and a readiness dashboard.

  1. Week 1: Pick one role and define the two AI tasks staff may safely perform.
  2. Week 2: Set the data boundary and write three fictional scenarios.
  3. Week 3: Run one sandbox exercise and capture common failure points.
  4. Week 4: Publish the capability tracker and manager sign-off card.

Next step: Run the 45-Minute AI Role Readiness Sprint

Pick one role, define the two AI tasks staff may safely perform, set the data boundary, build one fictional scenario, define the pass criteria, and publish the first capability tracker.
