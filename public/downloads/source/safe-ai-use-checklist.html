The four moves

Banker AI safety is not a 200-page policy. It is four habits, practiced every time. If your team can do these four things in their sleep, you have closed 90% of your exposure on day one.

1 · Strip sensitive data

  • Remove customer names, account numbers, balances, transaction details, Social Security numbers, addresses, and dates of birth.
  • Replace real identifiers with placeholders such as [CUSTOMER], [ACCOUNT TYPE], [DATE], [AMOUNT].
  • Do not paste confidential bank records, examiner correspondence, or vendor contracts into public AI tools.
  • If you would not email it to a stranger, do not paste it into a chat tool you do not control.

2 · Ask clearly

  • State the role you want the AI to play, the task, the context, the format, and the constraints.
  • Ask for a draft, a checklist, a summary, or a set of options — not a final decision.
  • Tell the AI what not to do, especially when the work is customer-facing or regulated.
  • If the first answer is wrong, change the prompt — do not bargain with the model.

3 · Fact-check outputs

  • Verify numbers, dates, citations, policy claims, and procedural steps against your source documents.
  • Mark anything uncertain with [VERIFY] and treat those items as unfinished work.
  • Keep human judgment responsible for the final use of the output.
  • If the AI cites a regulation, look up the regulation. The AI is not allowed to be your last source.

4 · Escalate risky decisions

  • Escalate legal, compliance, credit, privacy, complaint, and customer-impacting decisions.
  • Use approved internal tools and workflows for sensitive work — never a public chat tool.
  • When uncertain, classify the use case as higher risk and route it through a documented review.
