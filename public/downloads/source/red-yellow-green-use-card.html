The three lanes

Every use case your team brings to AI fits in one of three lanes. The lanes are not about what AI can do — they are about what your bank will let it do without a second pair of human eyes.

Green — usually safe with no sensitive data

  • Drafting internal memos that do not name customers.
  • Brainstorming training scenarios and discussion questions.
  • Summarizing public documents — FFIEC bulletins, regulator press releases, your own published policies.
  • Building reusable templates with [CUSTOMER], [ACCOUNT TYPE], [DATE] placeholders.

Rule: if you'd be comfortable putting it on a whiteboard in the break room, it is green.

Yellow — approved tool only, strip sensitive data, human review before use

  • Customer-facing draft language — emails, voicemail scripts, branch signage copy.
  • Internal summaries of policies or examiner correspondence.
  • Operational checklists and procedure drafts.
  • First-pass financial or risk analysis where AI is the calculator, not the decision-maker.

Rule: if a customer or examiner could read it, it is yellow. A qualified human signs off before it leaves the building.

Red — do not use public AI tools

  • Customer PII, NPI, account records, balances, transaction-level data.
  • Credit decisions, adverse action reasoning, or anything in the ECOA / Reg B path.
  • Legal, regulatory, or compliance determinations.
  • Authentication, fraud workflows, or any record tied to a security control.

Rule: if a regulator would expect a documented model under SR 11-7, it does not belong in a public chat tool.

The review rule

If an AI output could affect a customer, an examiner, a policy, a control, or a financial decision, a qualified human must review it before use. No exceptions.

What "qualified human review" means

  • The reviewer has the authority to sign off on the work in its non-AI form.
  • The reviewer compares the AI output against a known source — your policy, your data, your written procedure — not against memory.
  • The reviewer flags uncertain claims with [VERIFY] and does not pass them downstream.

Three questions to ask before you paste

  1. Whose data is in this prompt? If the answer involves a real customer, escalate or strip.
  2. Who is the audience for the output? Internal-only is yellow at worst. Customer or examiner is yellow with mandatory review.
  3. What happens if the AI is confidently wrong? If the answer is "we lose a customer," "we miss a control," or "we file an inaccurate report," classify it red and use an approved internal workflow.

When in doubt

Classify higher. A yellow use treated like a green use is how banks end up in front of examiners. A red use treated like a yellow use is how banks end up in the press. There is no penalty for being conservative with a tool your team is still learning.
