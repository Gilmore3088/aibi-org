You cannot govern what you have not listed. An AI use case inventory is
the plain, unglamorous spreadsheet that every other AI control depends on:
the policy points to it, vendor reviews feed it, the board report
summarizes it, and an examiner asks for it.

The term now has a shared definition. The AIEOG AI Lexicon, released in
February 2026 by Treasury and the financial sector, includes "AI use case
inventory" among its defined terms. And the CSBS AI Supervisory Framework
for state examiners names the AI inventory and use cases as one of its
core review areas.

## The fields that matter

Resist the urge to build a forty-column tracker. These ten fields answer
nearly every question a committee or examiner will ask:

## Find the AI you didn't know you had

The first pass always finds more than expected. Look in four places:

1. **Your vendor list.** Ask each technology vendor in writing whether
   their product uses AI or machine learning, and what it does. Fraud,
   BSA/AML monitoring, document capture, and digital banking are the
   usual suspects.
2. **Release notes.** Vendors are switching on AI features in routine
   updates. Someone should read them.
3. **Department heads.** Ask each one what tools their team uses to write,
   summarize, or analyze — including free ones. Make it clear this is a
   census, not a disciplinary sweep, or you will not get honest answers.
4. **Expense reports and network logs.** Subscriptions to AI tools on
   corporate cards, and traffic to AI sites, show what people actually use.

## Tier the risk simply

Three tiers are enough for a community institution:

- **High** — touches credit decisions, customer or member eligibility,
  BSA/AML outcomes, or acts without a person reviewing first.
- **Moderate** — uses restricted data or produces customer-facing content,
  with a person reviewing before release.
- **Low** — internal drafting and summarizing with no restricted data.

The tier sets the review: high-tier uses get documented testing, a second
reviewer, and periodic re-review; low-tier uses get recorded and spot
checked. CSBS publishes an optional AI Use Case Risk Tiering Worksheet
with the framework if you want a more detailed structure.

## Keep it alive

An inventory goes stale the month after it is built unless updating it is
part of something that already happens. Attach it to three existing
moments: new tool approval under your AI policy, vendor onboarding and
renewal in your third-party program, and the annual policy review. Give
one person ownership and a quarterly reminder.

## What to do this month

Start the list with what you already know, even if it is five rows. Send
the vendor letter. Name the owner. A short, honest inventory is worth more
than a complete one that never gets finished — and it is the first
document to have ready if your examiner asks.
