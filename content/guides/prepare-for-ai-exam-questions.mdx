There is no comprehensive AI-specific banking regulation. GAO's 2025
review found that regulators primarily oversee AI through existing laws,
regulations, guidance, and risk-based examinations. In practice that means AI questions show up inside reviews
you already know — information security, third-party risk, compliance
management, and governance — rather than as a separate exam.

What changed in 2026 is that the questions became easier to predict. The
CSBS Artificial Intelligence Supervisory Framework gives state examiners
a Core Examiner Guide with initial scoping questions, a document request
list, and procedures for governance and oversight, the AI inventory and
use cases, and generative AI. It is discretionary — each state decides
how to use it — but it is public, and you can read it before your examiner does. It is
written for state-chartered banks and state-licensed nonbanks; if a federal
agency is your primary regulator, treat it as a public preview of the
questions rather than your examiner's checklist.

## The five documents to have ready

1. **AI use case inventory.** Every AI use, built, bought, or embedded in
   vendor software, with an owner and a risk tier.
   [How to build one](/guides/ai-use-case-inventory-bank).
2. **AI policy.** Approved tools, the data line, human review, and how
   new uses are approved — including generative AI specifically.
   [What to include](/guides/ai-acceptable-use-policy-community-bank).
3. **Vendor list with AI flagged.** For each vendor whose product uses AI,
   the due diligence on file and any contract terms on data use.
4. **Training records.** Who has been trained on the policy and when.
5. **Governance trail.** Committee minutes or board reports showing that
   management and the board know what AI is in use and how it is
   controlled.

If one of these does not exist, that is your work plan.

## Run a dry run

Have someone in risk or compliance answer the scoping questions on paper
as if the examiner were asking them. Practical questions to rehearse:

- What AI does the institution use today, and how do you know the list is
  complete?
- Who approves a new AI tool or use, and where is that recorded?
- What data are employees allowed to put into AI tools? How do you know
  they follow that?
- Which AI uses affect customers or members directly? How is output
  reviewed before it reaches them?
- Which vendors provide AI, and what did your due diligence find?
- How does the board hear about AI risk?

Write the answers down. Where an answer is "we don't know," you have
found a finding before the examiner did.

## Separate models from generative AI

Examiners may treat traditional models and generative AI differently,
and so should your documentation. SR 26-2, the April 2026 model risk
guidance, is non-binding and leaves generative and agentic AI outside its
formal scope. The CSBS framework, by contrast, gives generative AI its own
review area. An institution whose AI documentation stops at credit and
BSA models will have a visible gap.
[More on SR 26-2 and generative AI](/guides/sr-26-2-community-banks-generative-ai).

## What good looks like

For a community bank or credit union, a strong answer is not a large
program. It is a short, current inventory; a policy staff can actually
follow; vendor files with the AI questions answered; and evidence that
leadership is paying attention. A small program that is real is easier to defend than a large one that
exists only on paper.
