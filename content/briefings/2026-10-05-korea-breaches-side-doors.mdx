**What happened.** On October 2, Korea's Financial Services Commission called
an emergency meeting with the Financial Supervisory Service, the Financial
Security Institute, major banks, and card companies after a September 30 data
leak at Shinhan Bank and further cyberattacks on
Kookmin Bank and other large financial firms. Its notice gave the industry
three instructions. Find every IT system reachable from the internet and check
its vulnerabilities and access controls, regardless of whether it serves
customers or what kind of service it is. Close any path that reaches internal
information without authentication, especially lookups of personal data. And
share attacker IP addresses and methods quickly. The FSC will issue a
vulnerability checklist and require firms to report their self-inspection
results. It says it is still analyzing how the attacks worked; its notice does
not attribute them to AI.

**Why it matters to you.** Read the first instruction again: *regardless of
whether it serves customers.* Your online and mobile banking get tested, by
your digital-banking provider, your core vendor, and your annual penetration
test. The systems that slip through are the others: a portal for loan brokers
or auto dealers, a staff web app, a vendor-hosted rate or application page
under your name, an old test site nobody turned off. They often touch customer
data, and they rarely have an owner in the inventory.

That gap matters more as finding it gets cheaper. On September 30, Google
announced Gemini 4 Argon and released it first to vetted cyber defenders. It
reports that on a black-box test of live web systems, the model beat its
predecessor at discovering the attack surface, identifying vulnerabilities,
and producing proof-of-concept evidence. Google built it for defense and says
it is strengthening safeguards specifically to keep bad actors from using it
for cyberattacks. That tells you the capability cuts both ways. Mapping an
institution's forgotten doors is becoming automated work.

**What to do with it.** Three moves this week:

1. **Ask for the full list.** Have IT or your managed service provider list
   every system reachable from the internet, including vendor-hosted pages
   that carry your name. If the answer is only the customer channels, the
   list is incomplete.
2. **Test the lookups.** For each system that touches customer data, confirm
   that no path returns it without authentication. That is the exact weakness
   the FSC told Korean firms to hunt.
3. **Ask your testers what they use.** Ask your penetration tester or security
   provider whether their external attack-surface work now uses AI-assisted
   tools. The tooling on both sides is improving; your annual test should
   keep pace.

Korea's regulator is still investigating. You don't need its final report to
run its checklist.
