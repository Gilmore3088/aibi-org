**What happened.** On September 16, the Conference of State Bank Supervisors
released its Artificial Intelligence Supervisory Framework — a resource for
state examiners reviewing AI use at state-chartered banks and state-licensed
nonbank financial institutions. It is discretionary: it creates no new legal
requirements, and each state decides how to use it. Its Core Examiner Guide
lays out how an examiner approaches AI at an institution: initial scoping
questions, a document request list, and procedures covering governance and
oversight, the AI inventory and use cases, and generative AI and emerging
uses. The framework builds on the NIST AI RMF, the financial sector's
FS AI RMF, and Treasury's AI Lexicon.

**Why it matters to you.** Most community banks are state-chartered, which
makes this the most direct answer yet to the question every leadership team
has been asking: *what will our examiner actually ask about AI?* Until now
that was inference from non-binding federal frameworks. Now the examiner's
own guide — the questions, the documents, the review areas — is public. And
note what the guide centers on: governance, an AI inventory, and generative
AI specifically. That last one matters because SR 26-2, the interagency
model-risk guidance, is non-binding and leaves generative AI outside its
formal scope — the state framework walks right into the space the federal
guidance left open.

**What to do with it.** Three moves, all preparation rather than new systems:

1. **Read the Core Examiner Guide before your examiner does — or at least
   before your next exam.** Have someone on your risk or compliance team
   answer its scoping questions on paper now, as a dry run.
2. **Pre-stage the document request list.** An AI use inventory, your AI or
   acceptable-use policy, and the vendor list with AI features flagged cover
   most of it. If those don't exist yet, this is the forcing function.
3. **Give generative AI its own page.** The framework treats it as a distinct
   review area, so an institution whose AI documentation stops at models in
   the SR 26-2 sense has a visible gap.

A discretionary framework is not a mandate. It is something better: advance
notice of the conversation.
