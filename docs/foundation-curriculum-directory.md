# Foundation Course — Curriculum Directory

**Course:** AiBI-Foundation · The AI Banking Institute
**Audience:** community-bank and credit-union staff with little or no prior AI use
**Promise:** safe professional confidence with AI in real banking work
**Format:** 12 self-paced modules, completion-based, ungraded, tool-agnostic
**Pricing tiers:**
- **Free, no login** — Module 1
- **Free, email gate** — Modules 2–3
- **Paid · $295 lifetime** — Modules 4–12 (the sandbox debuts at M4)

## Module 1 — AI for Your Workday

- **Purpose:** dissolve fear. The free hook. Stand-alone value in ~12 minutes.
- **Format:** ~12–15 min · no sandbox (demonstration only).
- **Key lessons:**
  - You already do this work (replying to unclear emails, summarizing meetings, rewriting things to sound professional). AI just helps you do it faster.
  - There are basically two kinds of AI helper: **Writing & Thinking** (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) and **Research & Answers** (Perplexity).
  - You don't need all of them. Most people start with one.
  - Plain English works — no programmer voice required.
  - One safety rule, gently: never paste customer names, account numbers, or member data into a public AI tool.
- **Tools referenced:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity (named only as illustrative examples).
- **Outcomes:** the learner can name two kinds of AI tools, can read a side-by-side "messy email → clear email" transformation, and has one safety rule in their head.
- **Takeaway items (cards saved to the Toolbox):**
  - **Rewrite Card** — *"Rewrite this internal email so the action, owner, and deadline are crystal clear."*
  - **Summarize Card** — *"Summarize this meeting into key decisions, action items, and next steps."*

## Module 2 — What AI Is and Is Not

- **Purpose:** build *calibrated* judgment. The progression from "AI can help me" → "AI still needs me."
- **Format:** ~12–15 min · no sandbox (guided demonstration + micro-checks).
- **Key lessons:**
  - AI sounds exactly as confident when it's uncertain as when it's correct.
  - The recurring metaphor: AI is a confident new hire who writes well but doesn't truly understand. Your job is to check it.
  - AI is safest when it's working from material you gave it — riskier when it's recalling facts from memory.
  - You become good at *reviewing* AI, not at trusting it.
  - The four-check review reflex (ten seconds): **Numbers · Dates · Names & citations · Policy, compliance, or regulatory claims.**
- **Tools referenced:** none new. The worked example uses a generic chat surface for the FDIC/NCUA hallucination demo (AI confidently says FDIC insures credit unions; correct answer is NCUA).
- **Outcomes:** the learner can recognize the shape of an AI hallucination, knows the four classes of claim that always deserve review, and has a card that makes AI surface its own uncertainty.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Trust-Check Card** — *"Point out anything here that may be incorrect, outdated, uncertain, or worth verifying before I use or share it — especially numbers, dates, names, citations, or policy/compliance claims."*

## Module 3 — Prompting Fundamentals

- **Purpose:** give the learner agency. Better inputs → better outputs. Last free module.
- **Format:** ~12–15 min · no sandbox (sandbox deliberately deferred to M4). Taught as plain English — not "TCREI," not "prompt engineering."
- **Key lessons:**
  - The real beginner barrier isn't understanding LLMs — it's "I tried it once and got generic garbage."
  - A good request has four plain parts: **Say what you want · Give the background · Show what good looks like · Refine what comes back.**
  - Asking and reviewing are two separate habits. The recipe is only the asking; the M2 four-check is downstream.
  - You don't write the perfect prompt — you steer. First response gets you 70–80%. One follow-up gets the rest.
- **Tools referenced:** a generic chat surface. Side-by-side demo: a vague "Write a note about a check hold" vs. the same task using the recipe (task + background + tone + length).
- **Outcomes:** the learner can turn a vague request into a clear one and knows how to fix a weak result with one follow-up.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Better-Prompt Recipe** — *Say what you want · Give the background · Show what good looks like · Refine what comes back* (then run the M2 four-check before you send).

## Module 4 — Your AI Work Profile *(The Workbench)*

- **Purpose:** activation. Cross the observer → participant threshold. First paid module; the sandbox debuts here.
- **Format:** ~15–20 min · sandbox = YES (guided, low-risk, impossible to fail).
- **Key lessons:**
  - You've already done the hard part — the thinking. What's left is pressing a button.
  - The first task is a transformation, never a creation (low creativity pressure, obvious success).
  - Tool fit is about *comfort and personality,* not benchmarks. Claude — conversational and thoughtful. ChatGPT — balanced and structured. Gemini — concise and direct.
  - There's no "best" AI — there's the one that sounds most like you. You're not marrying a tool; switch any time.
- **Tools used in the sandbox:** real markdown responses from Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini for the same prompt. Four labs run in the Workbench: **Data · Compliance · Loan · Ops** — each with banker-context review tags.
- **Outcomes:** the learner has actually used AI on a banking task end-to-end, compared the same task across tools, and picked a starting tool that fits how they work.
- **Takeaway items:**
  - **My AI Work Profile** — *My starting tool: ___ · What I'll use it for first: ___*
  - **Workbench Pack** — the actual work product saved per lab: source · prompt sent · AI response · review tags · follow-up prompt · improved response · adaptation idea.

## Module 5 — Teaching AI Your Style

*(Internal id: Projects and Context)*

- **Purpose:** repeatability. AI stops being single-use and becomes an ongoing working relationship.
- **Format:** ~15–20 min · sandbox returns (Workbench continues; escalation from "press Run" → "modify the reusable structure").
- **Key lessons:**
  - You shouldn't have to re-explain yourself to AI every morning. A **brief** is a reusable setup that tells AI how you work, what you care about, and what good looks like.
  - The safety trick: **placeholders** — `[first name]`, `[amount]`, `[branch]`. Nothing sensitive ever lives in the brief.
  - One brief, many situations. Same warm, consistent voice across check holds, debit-card delays, branch closures.
  - Reusable briefs should never contain real customer information.
- **Tools referenced:** Claude Projects, ChatGPT Custom Instructions, Gemini Gems, Microsoft Copilot context — exact paste paths shown without making the module a vendor tour.
- **Outcomes:** the learner has built a personal Brief with placeholders, watched it produce on-brand work across three different situations, and knows exactly where to paste it inside any AI tool.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Style + Context Brief** *(spec name: My First Project Brief)* — a reusable setup with placeholders; paste before a task, fill the blanks, run.

## Module 6 — Files & Document Workflows

- **Purpose:** cognitive relief. The first "this could materially reduce stress at work" module.
- **Format:** ~15–20 min · sandbox = constrained paste-in (no uploads — uploads feel permanent/risky and are deferred).
- **Key lessons:**
  - **Translator, not summarizer** — ask "what does this mean for me," not "summarize this." AI tells you what changed and what to do, not a shorter doc.
  - **Focused beats firehose** — paste the section that matters, not the whole file.
  - The safety test (now a course signature): **"Would I be fine if this snippet showed up outside the bank?"**
  - When in doubt: paste a smaller, redacted piece — or don't paste it at all.
- **Tools used in the sandbox:** a generic chat surface. The worked example is a made-up dense procedure paragraph; AI returns numbered, action-focused steps + a "confirm the exact threshold with your supervisor" nudge.
- **Outcomes:** the learner can paste a section of policy or procedure and ask AI what it means for their role, with a built-in escalation cue.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Document Workflow brief** — *"Here's a section of a policy/procedure. In plain terms, tell me what I need to do, what changed, and when to escalate."*

## Module 7 — Which Tool, When?

*(Internal id: AI Tools Landscape)*

- **Purpose:** reduce decision fatigue. "This module isn't more to learn — it's less to decide."
- **Format:** ~10–12 min · no sandbox (judgment module; the micro-check is the practice).
- **Key lessons:**
  - Categories first, vendors second. Routing rule of thumb:
    - **Writing / thinking** → ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
    - **Facts you can check the source of** → Perplexity
    - **Understand a long document** → the M6 document workflow
    - **Quick help inside Word, Excel, Outlook** → Copilot
  - The research tool isn't "more correct" — it just shows where the answer came from, so you can verify faster. Sources ≠ truth.
  - Switching tools isn't a mistake — it's how everyone actually works.
- **Tools referenced:** the named writing tools above; Perplexity as the research example; a side-by-side of a writing tool's confident-but-sourceless answer vs. a research tool's links-you-can-check.
- **Outcomes:** when a task lands, the learner knows which category to reach for — and knows there is no "best" AI.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Routing cheat sheet** — a one-glance "reach for this when…" reference (not a comparison chart).

## Module 8 — Your First Workflow

*(Internal id: Agents & Workflow Thinking)*

- **Purpose:** raise leverage one rung. Defuse the word "agent." Keep human checkpoints at the center.
- **Format:** ~13–15 min (the meatiest module) · sandbox = light (2-step run with a visible pause-for-confirmation gate).
- **Key lessons:**
  - A workflow is "a few steps strung together, with you checking at the right moments."
  - Most of what gets sold as an "agent" is just a few steps chained together. You don't need to build one — you need to know how to map one.
  - The five plain pieces: **what goes in → what AI does → where you check → the next step → when to stop and escalate.**
  - Signature line: **"The check-points are the point. They're where you stay in the chair."**
  - Map the steps before you hand any of them off. If you can't say the steps out loud, you're not ready to chain them.
  - Check-points don't go where they're convenient — they go where a mistake would leave the building.
- **Tools used in the sandbox:** a meeting-notes 2-step workflow (deliberately not the loan-doc one — lower stakes, easier to visualize the pause). AI pulls action items → pauses at a CHECK gate → after approval, drafts follow-up notes.
- **Outcomes:** the learner can take a small recurring task they own, map it as steps with checkpoints, and identify the escalation exit.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Workflow Map** — *What goes in · What AI does · Where I check · The next step · When I stop and escalate.*

## Module 9 — What's Safe to Share?

*(Internal id: Safe AI Use in Banking)*

- **Purpose:** the safety spine. Consolidate scattered safety habits into one calm instinct. Confident and safe, not anxious and restricted.
- **Format:** ~13–15 min · sandbox = light (the learner makes a risky task safe themselves — tap red details, swap them for placeholders, run).
- **Key lessons:**
  - Three zones, and most of your day is green:
    - **Green** — nothing about a real customer or member in it. Drafts, rewrites, summaries of general material, made-up examples. → just use it.
    - **Yellow** — internal but not about a specific person, or the specifics taken out. → approved tool, double-check, and when in doubt, make it greener.
    - **Red** — a real person's information, or a real decision about them. → stays out of public AI; use the bank's normal process.
  - The move: **strip the specifics, keep the structure.** You almost never have to give up the AI help — you give it the shape of the problem, not the person.
  - The test: **"Would I be fine if this showed up outside the bank?"** Green: yes. Red: absolutely not. Yellow: not sure — so make it greener.
- **Tools used in the sandbox:** a member-complaint task arrives with `[Dana Reyes]`, `[$4,000]`, `[account #3092]` highlighted in red. The learner taps each one to swap it for a placeholder, then runs the prompt. The output is a warm draft the learner finishes with real details offline.
- **Outcomes:** the learner can glance at any task and place it in green, yellow, or red, and knows how to convert most red tasks to green by abstracting the specifics.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Safe-Use card** — *Green = no real customer info → use it · Yellow = internal/sanitized → approved tool, make it greener · Red = a real person or decision → stays out, use the normal process · The move: strip the specifics, keep the structure · The test: "would I be fine if this showed up outside the bank?"*

## Module 10 — AI for Your Role

*(Internal id: Role-Based Use Cases)*

- **Purpose:** identity consolidation. AI stops being "AI tasks" and becomes just the work.
- **Format:** ~13–15 min · no sandbox (recognition + commitment module — the role selector and the "pick one task" are the interaction).
- **Key lessons:**
  - Almost all of it comes down to three recurring moments — the course-level anchors:
    - **"What matters here?"** → Document Workflow *(M6)*
    - **"Make it plain."** → Rewrite Card *(M1)*
    - **"Set it up once."** → Project Brief *(M5)*
  - Different desk, same three moves — and every one stays in the green.
  - Your work isn't special. Everyone's week is repetitive in the same few shapes, which is exactly why the same handful of cards works for all of you. You're not behind anyone.
  - You're not getting ready to do this — you can already do it.
- **Tools used:** no sandbox. The role selector (six roles shown — Frontline · Lending · Operations/Compliance · Leadership · IT/Technical · Marketing/Communications) renders the learner's seat only. The compositional walkthrough is an operations manager's Tuesday: procedure update → Document Workflow → Trust-Check → Rewrite Card → Project Brief.
- **Outcomes:** the learner can picture where AI fits into their actual week and has picked one real task on their desk for next week.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Role Card** — three sections: *My recurring moments · The cards I reach for · My first real-world win this week.* The first true "I have a system" Toolbox artifact.

## Module 11 — Your AI Toolkit

*(Internal id: Personal Prompt Library)*

- **Purpose:** the compounding-systems module. Course participant → system builder. The course will either become part of how the learner works or quietly slip away; M11 is where that's decided.
- **Format:** ~13–14 min · sandbox = light (the learner builds their *own* first card, impossible to fail).
- **Key lessons:**
  - You weren't given things to remember. You were given things to keep — and they're already in your hands.
  - The 11 cards built across M1–M10, shown grouped under warm, human names (not instructional categories):
    - **Handle the work** — Rewrite, Summarize, Document Workflow
    - **Stay in control** — Trust-Check, Safe-Use
    - **Stop repeating yourself** — Better-Prompt Recipe, Project Brief, Workflow Map
    - **Know your setup** — AI Work Profile, Routing Cheat Sheet, Role Card
  - The new course anchor — **"Do it twice? Save it."** A kit isn't finished — it compounds.
  - Repeatable beats impressive. The boring recurring tasks are exactly where a card pays you back.
  - Up until now, you've been learning from cards we gave you. This is the moment your kit starts becoming personal.
- **Tools used in the sandbox:** the learner picks a recurring task they already know is coming next week (weekly branch update, recurring customer follow-up, leadership recap, project status note, recurring meeting summary, or their own). They fill a four-line card: **Name it · When I'll use it · The prompt · One thing I'll never paste** (Safe-Use callback). The Toolkit surface comes to life — search, filter chips (★ Most used · This week · Favorites), three lanes.
- **Outcomes:** the learner has 11 prior cards plus their own card #12, organized inside a living Toolkit surface they will continue to use after the course ends.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **My Toolkit** — the living app surface itself: a titled bar ("12 cards · growing"), search, filter chips, Most-used and Added-this-week lanes, and an `+ add a card` affordance.

## Module 12 — Your First Monday

*(Internal id: Final Foundation Lab)*

- **Purpose:** normalization. The finale lets the learner *feel* the calm the course has been building — "this is just work now," not "I completed training."
- **Format:** ~15–18 min (the longest module, but still calm) · sandbox = fully guided (the learner runs one real task end-to-end, impossible to fail).
- **Key lessons:**
  - The real question was never whether you understood the course. It's whether you'll reach for any of this when a real task lands on your desk Monday morning.
  - One real task, all the way through. No new concepts.
  - The worked one-complete-pass — a member asks why their deposit is on hold:
    1. Routing (M7) → reach for a writing tool
    2. Project Brief (M5) → set it up with your warm member-reply brief
    3. Safe-Use (M9) → swap name + amount for placeholders
    4. Run → ~80% there
    5. Trust-Check (M2) → confirm the hold-release date
    6. Steer (M3) → tweak one line so it sounds like your branch
    7. Done → fill real details offline, send
  - Every single move was already in your kit. The finale doesn't add a skill; it reveals the kit was cumulative all along.
  - Recognition, not a grade. No score. *You don't freeze at a blank screen anymore · you know which tool to open and the move for the moment · you give it your ten-second check before you trust it · you know what stays out, without thinking hard.*
- **Tools used:** sandbox runs through the canonical seven-step pass; then the learner re-runs the same scaffold on their own recurring task (the M11 card).
- **Outcomes:** the learner has handled one real task end-to-end, in control, with nothing sensitive leaving the building. They leave with a certificate and a system.
- **Takeaway item:**
  - **Foundations Certificate** — *AiBI-Foundation · The AI Banking Institute · [name] can use AI thoughtfully, safely, and practically in real banking work — and has the system to keep getting better at it.* Quiet acknowledgement, not celebration theater. Editorial ledger styling, no gold seal, no badge.
  - **Your Toolkit, carried forward** — the M11 surface continues to grow each time the learner saves a card past the course.

## Cross-cutting design rules

These hold across all 12 modules and are why the course feels different from a generic prompting class:

- **Recognition before explanation; observe before perform.** Every module opens with a workplace moment the learner already has, not with a concept.
- **Low visual density.** Breathable type, progressive disclosure, no comparison matrices. Calmness is pedagogy.
- **Sandbox is core but selective.** Used only where playing teaches something; otherwise the work lives in the Toolbox. M1, M2, M3, M7, M10 are demonstration-only.
- **Tool-agnostic.** Named vendors appear as illustrative examples; the curriculum teaches transferable workflows, not vendor dependence.
- **Realistic synthetic, never real.** All banking material uses placeholders; no PII patterns; no fake names paired with account numbers.
- **Calm tone holds.** No hype, no "supercharge," no "unlock," no AGI / replace-your-job framing, no influencer voice. The signature style is editorial and low-ego.
- **Italics retired site-wide.** Emphasis is carried by color and weight, not slant.
- **Completion-based, ungraded.** No quizzes that block progress, no graded capstone, no proficiency exam. The certificate fires on full module completion.
