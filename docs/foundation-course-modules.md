# Foundation Course — Module Design Log

Canonical, evolving record of each module's **locked** learner-experience design. Drafts are iterated visually (brainstorm companion); once approved, the frozen design is recorded here with a lock date. Build against the LOCKED specs.

**Governing principles** (see also memory `project-foundation-course-design` + `Plans/foundation-course-unified-rebuild.md`):
- We build **safe professional confidence**, not "AI literacy." The job is reducing fear / granting emotional permission.
- **Tone is the signature style** — calm, low-ego, practical, believable workplace moments. No hype, jargon, AGI/"future of AI," "replace your job," or influencer/keynote voice. This tone must hold across all 12 modules (the #1 risk is tonal drift later).
- **Low visual density** — breathable, large type, progressive disclosure, no comparison matrices. Calmness is pedagogy.
- **Recognition before explanation; observe before perform.**
- **Sandbox is core but selective** — used only where playing teaches something; otherwise it lives in the Toolbox. Demonstration-only modules (like M1) don't embed it.
- **Completion-based, ungraded. Tool-agnostic** (teach transferable workflows, not vendor dependence).

## Module 1 — AI for Your Workday — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~12–15 min · **Sandbox:** none (demonstration only) · **Job:** dissolve fear; create a comfortable first-time user.
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you feel like AI is for someone like you, you know which handful of tools exist and which one to start with, and you've watched plain English turn a messy email into something clear — safely.*

**Step 1 — Feel seen first · "This is already your day."**
Open with recognition, not explanation. The learner's real day:
- replying to unclear emails
- summarizing meetings you barely had time to attend
- rewriting something so it sounds professional
- figuring out how to say something carefully
- turning scattered thoughts into a clear response

Reframe line: **"You already do this work. AI just helps you do it faster."**

**Step 2 — Get oriented, simply · "What's out there — just two kinds."**
A mental model, not a product list. Two buckets:
- **Writing & Thinking** — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot (draft, clean up, summarize, think out loud)
- **Research & Answers** — Perplexity (answers with sources you can check)

Settle line: *"these are basically two flavors of helper, not a pile of products to memorize."*
Reassurance: **"You don't need all of them. Most people start with one — we'll help you pick yours."**
Micro-interaction (no wrong answer): *"Which sounds most useful for your work right now?"* — Writing help / Research / Working with documents / Microsoft integration.
> Internal principle (NEVER in learner copy): tools that read your own uploaded files (e.g. NotebookLM) are deliberately held back until a later module — they invite uploading sensitive files before the safe-use habit is set. Do not explain the absence; silence is cleaner.

**Step 3 — See the magic · side-by-side (the heart of the module).**
A believable, rushed banking email → what AI turns it into. No typing, no sandbox.
- *Before:* "hey team following up from this morning, can someone check whether we got the updated docs from Henderson yet because underwriting still doesn't have everything they need and we're trying to keep this moving thanks"
- *After:* Subject: Henderson loan file — updated docs needed today. / Team — Underwriting is still missing documentation for the Henderson loan file. Please confirm whether the updated documents were received. / Owner: Dana · Deadline: Today by 3 PM / If anything is still outstanding, flag it to me immediately so we can keep the process moving. / Thanks.

Two lines that do enormous work:
- **"You do not need to talk to AI like a programmer. Plain English works."** (keep forever)
- Permission to start badly: **"The first response usually gets you 70–80% of the way there. Most people then ask one follow-up question to refine it — you can retry as many times as you want, and you can't break anything."**

**Step 4 — One habit, gently · "One important habit before you start."**
Soft transition, then: *"Public AI tools are powerful, but they are not where customer or member information belongs."*
Rule (one, not a compliance module): **Never paste customer names, account numbers, or any member data into a public AI tool. Use internal, non-sensitive examples only.** (Grows into green/yellow/red later.)

**Step 5 — Take it with them · two reusable cards** (behavioral anchors, platform-independent):
- **Rewrite Card** — "Rewrite this internal email so the action, owner, and deadline are crystal clear."
- **Summarize Card** — "Summarize this meeting into key decisions, action items, and next steps."
Saved to the Toolbox to paste into whatever tool they picked. (Live cross-model play is the optional Toolbox sandbox, not here.)

**Close — identity reinforcement:** *"You've now used AI the same way most professionals do: to make communication clearer, faster, and easier to act on."*

## Module 2 — What AI Is and Is Not — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~12–15 min · **Sandbox:** none (guided demonstration + micro-checks) · **Job:** introduce caution without fear; build *calibrated confidence*.
- **Progression:** M1 "AI can help me" → **M2 "AI still needs me / I know how to use it responsibly."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you understand the handful of things AI gets wrong, you've watched one happen, you've caught one yourself, and you have a ten-second review habit that keeps you in control — so you trust your judgment, not the tool's confidence.*
- **Format note:** behavior-centered — the learner identifies risk, chooses what deserves review, watches uncertainty surface, and rehearses the review reflex (not just passive explanation).

**Step 1 — Feel seen first · "It will sound certain even when it's wrong."**
Names the real workplace risk: confidence without certainty. *"It sounds exactly as confident when it's uncertain as when it's correct."* Reframe (the module's recurring metaphor): **"It's a confident new hire who writes well but doesn't truly understand what it's saying — or know when it's wrong."** Your job is to check it, like a junior's first draft.
Micro-interaction: *"Have you ever caught AI saying something confidently wrong?"* → payoff after answering: **"That's normal. The goal isn't to become suspicious of AI — it's to become good at reviewing it."** (Normalizes learning, removes shame.)

**Step 2 — Mental model, plain · "What it actually is (and isn't)."**
**"AI is more like a confident new hire than an encyclopedia. It's built to generate believable language, not guarantee truth."** Two columns — *Great at* (rephrasing, structuring, summarizing what you give it) vs *Slips on* (numbers & thresholds; dates, names & citations; policy, compliance, or regulatory claims). Key principle: **"AI is safest when it's working from material you gave it — and riskier when it's trying to recall facts from memory."**
Second micro-check (the instructional center): *"Which response deserves the closest review?"* — rewrite / summarize / list action items / **explain the regulation threshold** → reveal: the regulation question; AI is strongest transforming what you provided, weakest recalling facts. (Learner performs the judgment → sticky.)

**Step 3 — See it happen · believable, low-stakes banking hallucination.**
Q: "Which federal agency insures credit unions?" → AI confidently: "The FDIC insures both banks and credit unions." → The catch, shown visually: **Banks → FDIC · Credit unions → NCUA.** *"It didn't lie; it pattern-matched to something that sounded right."* Recurring lines: **"This is why you stay in the chair. The tool drafts; you decide."** + **"The people who get the most from AI aren't the ones who trust it most. They're the ones who check it fastest."** (Verification = expertise, not fear.)

**Step 4 — Practice the habit once · "Make AI help you review itself."** (the behavioral core)
Paste the Trust-Check Card after the wrong answer → the answer changes to surface its own uncertainty ("I may be incorrect here. Credit unions are federally insured by the NCUA…"). Mature takeaway: **"The follow-up didn't magically make AI perfect. It made the uncertainty visible. That's the habit."** (Teaches: better prompting improves *reviewability*, not guaranteed truth.)

**Step 5 — The one review reflex · four checks (ten seconds).**
**① Numbers ② Dates ③ Names & citations ④ Policy, compliance, or regulatory claims.** *"This isn't distrust — it's the same review you'd give a junior analyst's draft. It's what keeps you the decision-maker in the room."*

**Step 6 — Leave with one durable behavior.**
Trust-Check Card saved to the Toolbox — a repeatable review habit across any AI tool. **"The goal is not blind trust. The goal is faster verification."**

**Card kept — Trust-Check Card:** *"Point out anything here that may be incorrect, outdated, uncertain, or worth verifying before I use or share it — especially numbers, dates, names, citations, or policy/compliance claims."*

**Close — identity:** *"You're not someone who blindly trusts AI — and not someone afraid of it. You use it and you check it. That's what good judgment with AI looks like."*

## Module 3 — Prompting Fundamentals — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~12–15 min · **Sandbox:** none (guided demonstration + micro-checks; sandbox deliberately deferred to M4–5) · **Job:** give the learner agency — "I can drive this."
- **Progression:** M1 confidence → M2 judgment → **M3 control** ("better inputs create better outputs"). Every module reinforces human agency, not replacement.
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you can turn a vague request that gets you generic junk into a clear one that gets a usable result the first time — using a simple recipe that works on any tool.*
- **Framing rules:** taught as **plain English, no acronym** (NOT "TCREI" — keep it common-sense, not methodology). **Asking and reviewing are separate habits** — this recipe is only the asking; the Module 2 four-check review is downstream. Don't teach "prompt engineering"/magic syntax — teach communication, context, steering, refinement.

**Step 1 — Feel seen · "I tried it and it wasn't that helpful."**
The real beginner barrier isn't "I don't understand LLMs" — it's "I tried it once and got generic garbage." Key insight (teaches context *socially*, not technically): **"We asked the way we'd ask a coworker who already knows the situation. AI doesn't know your situation — until you tell it."** Reframe: **"Getting good results isn't a talent. It's a habit of saying a little more up front."**

**Step 2 — The recipe, plain · "A good request has four plain parts."** (a checklist you stop needing in a week, not a formula)
1. **Say what you want** — the actual task (draft / summarize / rewrite)
2. **Give the background** — what it needs to know (non-sensitive only)
3. **Show what good looks like** — tone, length, format
4. **Refine what comes back** — one follow-up to fix what's off
*"You won't always need all four, but when a result comes back weak, one of these is almost always the missing piece."* Then the separation: **"Before you use or share it, run your four-check review from Module 2. Asking and reviewing are two separate habits — this recipe is just the asking."**

**Step 3 — See it happen · side-by-side (strongest demo in the module).**
Task: a note to a customer about a hold on a deposited check.
- Vague ("Write a note about a check hold.") → cold, generic, corporate, says nothing useful.
- With the recipe (task + background: $4,000 check / new account / funds next Tuesday; + "warm but professional, ~4 sentences, no jargon") → a warm, specific, ready-to-send note.
Key line: **"Same tool. Same model. The only thing that changed was how you asked."** (Kills the myth that better output requires a more advanced AI.)

**Step 4 — Micro-check · "Spot the missing piece."**
Weak prompt: "Summarize this for the board." → *What would help most?* (length/format · give it the document · what the board cares about · all of the above) → reveal: **"All of the above — but if you only do one thing, give it the material to work from."** (Callback to M2: AI is safest working from what you give it. This grounding principle is becoming a course-wide thesis.)

**Step 5 — Practice once · "You don't write the perfect prompt — you steer."** (emotional center)
First result ~80% (a touch formal) → one plain follow-up ("make it warmer, cut to 3 sentences") → ready. **"You get close, then steer — like editing a junior's first draft. The first response gets you 70–80%; the follow-up gets you the rest."** (Kills perfectionism / prompt-anxiety; recurring line across M1–3.)

**Step 6 — Leave with one durable behavior · the recipe card.**
**"The goal isn't a perfect prompt. It's a usable result, faster."**

**Card kept — Better-Prompt Recipe:** *Say what you want · Give the background · Show what good looks like · Refine what comes back* (then run your Module 2 four-check review before you send).

**Close — identity:** *"You're not 'a natural with AI.' You just know how to ask — which means you can get a useful result whenever you need one, on any tool."*

## Module 4 — Your AI Work Profile — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~12–15 min · **Sandbox:** YES — **first appearance** (guided, low-risk, impossible to fail) · **First PAID module** (paywall lands here).
- **Job:** activation — cross the observer → participant threshold without anxiety. **Progression:** M1 confidence → M2 judgment → M3 control → **M4 "I can actually do this myself."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you've actually used AI yourself — run the same task in a couple of tools, felt the difference, and picked the one that fits how you work.*
- **Sandbox-debut rules:** prompt pre-written, data fake, we fund it, nothing to break, no one watching. First task is a **transformation** task (low creativity pressure, obvious success), never a creation task. Tool choice framed by **comfort/personality, not benchmarks**; no declared winner, no feature matrix.

**Step 1 — Feel seen · "Okay, but I still haven't actually done anything."**
Acknowledge the tension directly. **"You've already done the hard part — the thinking. What's left is pressing a button."** + **"There's nothing to break, nothing to get wrong, and no one watching."** (Neutralizes performance/judgment fear.)

**Step 2 — Meet the Sandbox, gently.** A safe place to try it, on us. Reassurance chips: ✓ made-up data only · ✓ we pay for it · ✓ prompt pre-written · ✓ nothing to break. Privacy line: **"No real customer or member information is used here, saved here, or needed here."** → **"You literally cannot get this wrong."**

**Step 3 — Press Run · the debut task.** Transform messy (believable) meeting notes → clear action items. Everything pre-loaded; they just run it. **Output is intentionally a little imperfect** ("a couple of these may need an owner confirmed") so learners expect *usable drafts, not robotic precision* (reinforces AI-drafts/human-finalizes). *"You did that."*

**Step 4 — Discover your fit · same task, different tools.** Run the same prompt in Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini. Light personality labels only: **Claude — conversational & thoughtful · ChatGPT — balanced & structured · Gemini — concise & direct.** Micro-check: *"Which felt most natural to you?"* → reveal: **"There's no 'best' AI — there's the one that sounds most like you. And you're not marrying a tool here — switch anytime. This is just a comfortable starting point."** (Reversibility kills commitment anxiety.)

**Step 5 — Make it yours · constrained personalization.** "Pick one of these — or type your own": customer follow-up email · meeting summary · action-item list · polite rewrite · branch announcement. Run again, watch it adapt. **"This is the steering from Module 3 — now you're doing it yourself."** Gentle continuity: *"Practice with made-up or non-sensitive examples only."* Payoff: **"You just did in five minutes what most people are still nervous to try."**

**Step 6 — Leave with your profile.** Identity-based artifact (creates commitment/personalization).

**Card kept — My AI Work Profile:** *My starting tool: ___ · What I'll use it for first: ___* (saved to Toolbox).

**Close — identity:** *"You're not someone who's read about AI anymore. You're someone who's used it."*

## Module 5 — Teaching AI Your Style *(internal id: Projects and Context)* — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~12–15 min · **Sandbox:** light return (escalation from M4's "press Run" → "modify the reusable structure") · **Job:** introduce **repeatability** — AI becomes workflow, not novelty.
- **Title note:** learner-facing title renamed from "Projects and Context" (too abstract) to **"Teaching AI Your Style."** Internal/code id unchanged.
- **Progression:** M1 approachable → M2 judgment → M3 better outputs → M4 use it myself → **M5 "I can make AI reusable and repeatable for the work I do over and over."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you'll have a reusable "brief" you can drop in front of any AI for a task you do over and over — so you stop re-explaining yourself every time, and you never type anything sensitive.*
- **Restraint rule (important):** teach repeatable-setup behavior WITHOUT enterprise jargon — never say reusable prompt / system prompt / workspace / context window / persistent instruction. Call it **a brief**. The value framed as *consistency*, not just saved typing.

**Step 1 — Feel seen · "You're re-explaining yourself every single time."**
Reframe AI from single-use tool → ongoing working relationship: **"You shouldn't have to re-explain yourself to AI every morning. People who get real leverage set it up once."**

**Step 2 — The idea, plain.** A **brief** = *"a reusable setup that tells AI how you work, what you care about, and what good looks like"* — pasted in before your task. The safe + reusable trick: **placeholders** (blanks like `[first name]`, `[amount]`) — nothing sensitive ever lives in it. Continuity: *"a brief just saves the 'background' and 'what good looks like' from Module 3 — write them once, not every time."*

**Step 3 — See it happen.** Cold-every-time vs. one saved brief (shown line-by-line, human/writable — not engineered). Fill blanks (`[a hold on a $4,000 check]` · `[Jordan]`) → on-brand note. **"Same quality every time, far less effort — and you never typed anything real."** (Quietly teaches consistency / standardization — an enterprise value, in approachable language.)

**Step 4 — Micro-check · "Which brief is safe to save?"** Brief A (real name + account #) vs Brief B (placeholders) → B. Reveal (aligned to M1 safety): **"Reusable briefs should never contain real customer information — saved templates and practice always use placeholders or non-sensitive examples."**

**Step 5 — Practice once · one brief → many situations.** In the sandbox: keep the brief, pick a different situation (radio: check hold / debit-card replacement delay / branch closure) → **the output changes to match, in the same warm voice** (demonstrates reuse, not repetition). **"That's the real payoff: one brief, many situations… every note comes out in the same warm, consistent voice. A setup you can reuse Monday."**

**Step 6 — Keep the first Project Brief** (start of a small reusable collection).

**Card kept — My First Project Brief:** a reusable setup with placeholders; paste before a task, fill the blanks, run.

**Close — identity:** *"You're not just using AI anymore — you're setting it up to work the way you do."* (Shift: user → director.)

## Module 6 — Files & Document Workflows — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~12–15 min · **Sandbox:** constrained paste-in (NO uploads yet — they feel permanent/risky) · **Job:** cognitive relief — the first "this could materially reduce stress at work" module; highest perceived value so far.
- **Progression:** … M5 reusable → **M6 "I can safely work through documents and extract what matters."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you can drop a chunk of a document in front of AI and ask what it actually means for you — instead of skimming a long file and hoping you didn't miss the part that matters.*
- **Signature framings introduced (carry forward aggressively):** **"Translator, not summarizer"** (AI tells you what changed + what to do, not a shorter doc) · **"Focused beats firehose"** (paste the section that matters — teaches focused context + safety without the word "chunking").

**Step 1 — Feel seen · "It's 4:55, and you're skimming a six-page procedure."** Painfully specific document-fatigue moment. Reframe: **"You don't have to skim blindly anymore."** Calm, no hype (never "analyze 900 pages instantly").

**Step 2 — The idea, plain.** AI is strongest from material you give it; a document is just more material. Two habits: **(1) paste the section that matters, not the whole file** (focused beats firehose); **(2) ask "what does this mean for me," not "summarize this"** → **"a translator, not a summarizer. You don't want shorter — you want what changed and what you do about it."**

**Step 3 — See it happen.** A realistically dense/bureaucratic (made-up) procedure paragraph → *"what does a teller actually need to do differently?"* → action-focused answer + a "confirm the exact threshold with your supervisor" nudge (embeds M2 judgment). **"It didn't shorten the document. It told you what to do."**

**Step 4 — The boundary, practical (best safety framing in the course so far).** The plain test: **"Would I be fine if this snippet showed up outside the bank?"** Fine to paste (made-up/training examples · public-facing procedures · redacted excerpts w/ no customer/member info) vs. keep out of public tools (customer info, statements/full files, confidential/internal-only). Micro-check. **"When in doubt, paste a smaller, redacted piece — or don't paste it at all."** (Teaches proportionality / reduce exposure — not "AI dangerous.") Behavioral, not legalistic.

**Step 5 — Practice once, constrained.** Sandbox: a sample wire-transfer procedure snippet + plain question ("what are the exact steps I need to follow?") → numbered steps in natural phrasing + "if anything feels unusual, follow the normal escalation process." Optional refinement: "turn that into a checklist I can keep at my desk" (quietly teaches transformation/chaining). Made-up excerpts only. No uploads, no bring-your-own.

**Step 6 — Keep a Document Workflow brief** (a Module-5 brief built for documents — domain-specific reusable workflow, a leap in leverage with no added jargon).

**Card kept — Document Workflow brief:** *"Here's a section of a policy/procedure. In plain terms, tell me what I need to do, what changed, and when to escalate."*

**Close — identity:** *"You're not skimming and hoping anymore — you can ask a document exactly what it means for you."*

## Module 7 — Which Tool, When? *(internal id: AI Tools Landscape)* — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~10–12 min · **Sandbox:** none (a judgment module — the micro-check is the practice) · **Job:** reduce hesitation/decision-fatigue ("which one do I open?"), NOT teach tools.
- **Title note:** learner-facing renamed "AI Tools Landscape" → **"Which Tool, When?"** (the learner's real internal question; the old name sounded conference/consulting). Internal id unchanged.
- **Progression:** … M6 documents → **M7 "I know which tool to reach for, for which kind of work."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: when a task lands, you'll know which tool to reach for — without second-guessing, and without believing there's one "best" AI.*
- **Hard guardrails (this module's trap):** NO benchmarks, feature matrices, "best model," vendor enumeration, or tool fandom. Categories first, vendors second. Stay anchored in "what am I trying to do right now?"

**Step 1 — Feel seen · "…which one do I even open?"** The friction is decision fatigue, not understanding models. Key framing line (one of the course's strongest): **"This module isn't more to learn — it's less to decide."** (Protects against complexity accumulation; clarity rises, friction stays flat.)

**Step 2 — The idea, plain · "Reach for this when…"** A routing rule of thumb (categories first):
- writing/thinking → a conversational tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Copilot)
- facts you can check the source of → a research tool (Perplexity)
- understand a long document → the document workflow (M6)
- quick help inside Word/Excel/Outlook → Copilot ("it's already in there")
Plus the M4 callback: **"you're not marrying any of them… there's no single 'best' AI."**

**Step 3 — Feel the difference · wrong tool vs right tool.** A fact you can stand behind (current policy detail / market fact): a writing tool gives a confident, sourceless answer ("sounds just as sure when it's wrong" — M2; you'd verify anyway) vs a research tool gives links you can check. Critical framing protecting M2: **"The research tool isn't 'more correct' — it just shows where the answer came from, so you can verify faster."** (Verification acceleration, NOT truth delegation. Sources ≠ truth.)

**Step 4 — Micro-check · build the routing instinct.** Board figures needing visible sources → research tool. (Writing tool = confident, untraceable — the M2 problem.)

**Step 5 — Keep a one-glance routing cheat sheet** (not a comparison chart). Plus **"Not sure? Start with the one you already use most — you can always switch."** (removes tool paralysis) and **"switching tools isn't a mistake — it's how everyone actually works"** (fights AI tribalism; carries M4's "not marrying a tool").

**Close — identity:** *"You don't need the 'best' AI. You just need to know which one to open for the task in front of you — and now you do."*

## Module 8 — Your First Workflow *(internal id: Agents & Workflow Thinking)* — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~13–15 min (the meatiest module) · **Sandbox:** YES, light — a 2-step run with a *visible* human-checkpoint pause · **Job:** raise leverage one rung without breaking the calm.
- **Title note:** learner-facing renamed "Agents & Workflow Thinking" → **"Your First Workflow"** (practical, approachable, achievable; "agent" sounds enterprise/architecture-heavy, and "Stringing a Few Steps Together" undersells the value). The title's emotional job is *"you already know enough to do this."* Internal/code id unchanged (`m8-agents-workflow-thinking`).
- **Progression:** … M7 which tool → **M8 "I can design small AI-assisted workflows while staying in control."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you can take a small task you do over and over, map it as a few steps with your own check-points built in, and let AI carry the steps while you stay in control of the ones that matter.*
- **The trap this module avoids (the #1 risk in the back half):** this is where most AI education breaks — hype, "agents everywhere," automation theater, autonomous-AI fear, sci-fi. We defuse "agent" head-on as *"a few steps chained together"* and keep oversight, pauses, escalation, and human control at the center. **Scope = the individual only** — your own repeatable task. Team-wide / automatic-across-the-bank workflows are explicitly a deliberately light "bigger conversation for later" (future AiBI-S/L), no acronyms in copy.

**Step 1 — Feel seen first · "You run the same little shuffle every time."**
The painfully specific moment (a callback to the Henderson loan email that *opened Module 1*): documents land from a borrower and every time you do the same dance — open the email, dig up your checklist, cross-check what they sent, work out what's missing, write the same "we still need a few things" note. **"It's not hard. It's just repetitive — and when you're slammed, it's exactly where a step gets dropped."** Reframe (reframes workflows as *existing human behavior*, not new technical systems — this is why the module stays calm): **"You've been running a workflow in your head for years. AI just lets you set it down — so you stop carrying every step yourself."**

**Step 2 — The idea, plain · "A workflow is just a few steps, with you checking in between."**
One mental model, no jargon: **"A few steps strung together, with you checking at the right moments."** Then defuse the scary word directly: **"You'll hear the word 'agent' a lot. **Step 3 — See it happen · the document workflow, mapped.**
The recurring task from Step 1, laid out top-to-bottom with AI steps, two human ✋ CHECK gates (navy), and a red **→ Escalate** exit branch. Compounds the whole course (document interpretation, briefs, review habits, escalation, sequencing):
- **In:** a borrower's email + the documents they attached
- **AI step:** "Here's my checklist and what they just sent — what's still missing?" → a short outstanding-items list *(translator, not summarizer — M6)*
- **✋ You check:** is the list right? *(one flagged item is already on file — your checkpoint catches it; the four-check from M2)*
- **AI step:** using your saved brief *(M5)*, draft a warm note listing exactly what's still needed, with the deadline
- **✋ You check:** read it, fix anything, press send
- **→ Escalate:** if the docs reveal a *judgment* call (income doesn't match the application, a legal doc unsigned) that's not a "write the email" step — you stop and route it to underwriting
Output is intentionally a touch imperfect (the false-positive item) so the checkpoint earns its keep. Close line (tightened from the original "not a robot doing your job" — less defensive, more professional): **"AI ran the steps. You ran the check-points. That's a workflow: AI handles the repeatable steps, and you stay in control of the decisions that matter."**

**Step 4 — Micro-check · "Where does the check-point belong?"**
A 3-step workflow (AI reads an incoming customer email → drafts a reply → reply goes out). *"Where do you put your check-point?"* — only at the very end / wherever it's convenient / nowhere, the steps are simple / **before any step that acts on a customer or a decision.** Reveal (real governance thinking in plain language): **"Check-points don't go where they're convenient — they go where a mistake would leave the building."** (continuity with M6's "would I be fine if this showed up outside the bank?")

**Step 5 — Practice once · a 2-step run that stops for you (light sandbox).**
DELIBERATELY the meeting-notes workflow, NOT the loan-document one — keeps complexity, stakes, and cognitive load low (familiar, low-risk, easy to visualize the pause). AI pulls action items from messy notes → **the workflow pauses at a CHECK gate** ("confirm these three owners before we continue") → learner approves → AI turns the confirmed items into short follow-up notes. ## Module 9 — What's Safe to Share? *(internal id: Safe AI Use in Banking)* — LOCKED 2026-05-21

- **Length:** ~13–15 min · **Sandbox:** YES, light — the learner makes a risky task safe *themselves* (taps the red details, swaps them for placeholders, runs it) · **Job:** the safety spine. Consolidate the scattered safety habits into ONE calm instinct, framed so the learner leaves **confident and safe, not anxious and restricted.**
- **Title note:** learner-facing renamed "Safe AI Use in Banking" → **"What's Safe to Share?"** ("Safe AI Use in Banking" reads compliance-heavy/intimidating; the new title is the learner's *real* hesitation, practical and human). Internal/code id unchanged (`m9-safe-ai-use-banking`).
- **Progression:** … M8 workflows → **M9 "I know exactly what's safe to share, so I can use AI confidently without second-guessing."**
- **Goal (learner-facing):** *By the end: you can glance at any task and know instantly whether it's safe to drop into AI as-is, safe with a quick tweak, or needs to stay out — so you can use AI freely for the vast majority of your work, without second-guessing.*
- **The trap this module avoids (highest-stakes in the course):** this is the single hardest *emotional* module — the one most likely to re-inject fear, legal anxiety, risk theater, or policy overload and kill adoption momentum. The deliberate counter-move: **freeing, not fearful.** Green = "most of your day"; red = small + clear; **yellow stays deliberately light** (over-explaining yellow makes the module policy-heavy and collapses the tone). **No "SAFE" acronym or any branded framework** (banned in learner copy) — just three colors, one move, one test. The core reframe is safety as *abstraction*, not *restriction*.

**Step 1 — Feel seen first · "I'm never quite sure if I'm allowed to put this in."**
Names the quiet hesitation under the whole course: you go to use AI for something real and a little voice asks *"…wait, am I allowed to paste this?"* — so **"you either back off and lose the help, or you paste it and carry a small worry the rest of the day. Neither feels good."** (addresses the *anxiety* side most safety training ignores). Reframe: **"There's a simple line here — and once you can see it, the worry goes away. You'll also find most of your work sits comfortably on the safe side of it."**

**Step 2 — The idea, plain · "Three zones — and most of your day is green."**
Not a policy memo — three colors. Lead with green being big:
- **Green** — *nothing about a real customer or member in it.* Drafts, rewrites, summaries of public/general-internal material, brainstorming, made-up examples. → just use it (with the M2 review). **"This is most of your work."** (protect this line — it prevents fear-based withdrawal)
- **Yellow** — *internal but not about a specific person, or the specifics taken out.* Sanitized memos, aggregate numbers, no names. → approved tool, double-check, *and when in doubt, make it greener.* (kept light on purpose)
- **Red** — *a real person's information, or a real decision about them.* Names, account numbers, balances; credit/dispute/closure decisions; confidential or legal. → doesn't go into public AI; stop and use the bank's normal process.
The test that decides the color (now a full course signature — protect aggressively): **"Would I be fine if this showed up outside the bank?"** → **"Green: yes, fine. Red: absolutely not. Yellow: not sure — so make it greener."** (the M6 paste test, now the whole rule.)

**Step 3 — See it happen · "Strip the specifics, keep the structure."**
The move that makes this freeing instead of limiting. A red task almost never has to be abandoned — hand AI the *shape*, not the person. Worked red→green (Henderson callback): *"Write Mr. Henderson's adverse-action letter — denied for a 52% DTI on account #4471"* (RED) → *"Help me draft a template adverse-action letter for a loan declined on debt-to-income… I'll fill in the specifics myself"* (GREEN). Signature line (turns safety from restriction into abstraction — one of the course's strongest): **"You almost never have to give up the AI help. You just give it the shape of the problem, not the person."** Continuity: same trick as the M5 brief (placeholders), and AI is *better* at the template than the specific case (M2: strongest from structure you give it).

**Step 4 — Micro-check · "What's the safest move?"**
A member sends an angry fee email; you want AI's help with a calm reply. *What do you do?* — paste it all (name + account) / **paste it with name & account removed, keep the situation** / don't use AI at all / forward to compliance first. Reveal (teaches *sanitization as the default*, not avoidance): **"Strip the name and account, keep the situation. That turns it green and you keep the help."** You don't have to give up AI or escalate a routine reply — you just don't hand over the parts that identify the person.

**Step 5 — Practice once · the learner makes it safe themselves (light sandbox).**
Best sandbox design since M4: the learner performs the actual safety transformation. Made-up data, impossible to fail. A task arrives with details highlighted in red; **tap each one to swap it for a placeholder, then run.** *"Draft a follow-up to [Dana Reyes] about the hold on her [$4,000] check on account [#3092]"* → *"Draft a warm follow-up to [first name] about a hold on a [amount] check"* → a warm, ready note the learner finishes with real details offline. Emotional payoff (keeps safety from feeling expensive): **"Safety didn't cost you the help — it just changed three words."** → **"This is the habit that lets you use AI for real work, every day, without the worry."**

**Step 6 — Keep an artifact + identity close.**
Artifact = **Safe-Use card** (deliberately NOT over-designed — no acronyms, trees, or matrices). Framing: **"This isn't a new rule to memorize — it's the one you've been building since Module 1, now in a single picture."**

**Card kept — Safe-Use card:** *Green = no real customer info → use it · Yellow = internal/sanitized → approved tool, make it greener · Red = a real person or decision → stays out, use the normal process · The move: strip the specifics, keep the structure · The test: "would I be fine if this showed up outside the bank?"*

**Close — identity (balances confidence + caution without fear or recklessness — one of the strongest closes in the course):** *"You're not someone who's nervous about AI — and not someone careless with it. You know exactly what's safe, which means you can use it freely for almost everything you do."*
